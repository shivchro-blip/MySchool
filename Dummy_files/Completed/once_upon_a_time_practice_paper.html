Q1Synonym
Choose the correct synonym for the underlined word.
"They used to shake hands without hearts while their left hands search my empty pockets."
a)minds
b)souls
c)sincerity
d)warmth
"Hearts" in this context means genuine feeling or sincerity. "Sincerity" is the closest synonym — both refer to the quality of being genuinely felt rather than performed.
Q2Synonym
Choose the correct synonym for the underlined word.
"I have learned to wear many faces like dresses with all their conforming smiles."
a)charming
b)complying
c)comforting
d)confusing
"Conforming" means adjusting to meet expected standards or social norms. "Complying" is the closest synonym — both mean adapting to what is required or expected rather than acting from genuine feeling.
Q3Synonym
Choose the correct synonym for the underlined word.
"I want to unlearn all these muting things."
a)remember
b)practise
c)discard
d)ignore
"Unlearn" means to deliberately shed or get rid of something previously learned. "Discard" — to throw away or abandon — is the closest synonym in this context.
Q4Antonym
Choose the correct antonym for the underlined word.
"But now they only laugh with their teeth while their ice-block-cold eyes search behind my shadow."
a)frozen
b)warm
c)distant
d)hollow
"Ice-block-cold" describes eyes that are utterly without warmth or feeling. Its antonym is "warm" — expressing genuine kindness and sincere emotional engagement.
Q5Antonym
Choose the correct antonym for the underlined word.
"To say 'Glad to meet you' without being glad."
a)happy
b)joyful
c)sorrowful
d)sincere
"Glad" means happy or pleased. Its antonym is "sorrowful" — meaning sad or full of grief, the opposite emotional state.
Q6Antonym
Choose the correct antonym for the underlined word.
"They used to laugh with their hearts and laugh with their eyes: but now they only laugh with their teeth."
a)mouth
b)heart
c)soul
d)mind
In the poem, "teeth" represents superficial, fake laughter. Its antonym in context is "heart" — representing genuine, sincere emotion. The poem itself establishes this contrast: "laugh with their teeth" vs "laugh with their hearts."

Q9Prefix / Suffix

a)-ness
b)-ly
c)-ment
d)-tion
"Sincere" + "-ly" = "sincerely" — an adverb meaning in a genuine or honest manner. This is the most common and correct suffix for this adjective to form an adverb.
Q10Prefix / Suffix

b)un-
c)mis-
d)re-
"Un-" + "learn" = "unlearn" — a key word directly used in the poem meaning to deliberately shed something previously learned. "Re-" would give "relearn," which is also in the poem but is not the antonym of "learn."

Q12Syllabification

Q13Phobia / Mania

a)Dromomania
b)Katoptronophilia
c)Balloonomania

Katoptronophilia refers to an obsession with mirrors and one's own reflection — fittingly relevant to this poem in which the narrator confronts his own reflection in the mirror and sees his hollow smile.
Q14Preposition
Fill in the blank with the suitable preposition: "They search _____ my shadow."
a)in front of
b)behind
c)beside
d)within
The correct preposition from the poem is "behind" — "search behind my shadow." The hypocrites look behind the narrator, scanning for wealth or power, not at the person himself.
Q15Question Tag
Add a suitable question tag: "They used to laugh with their hearts, _____ ?"
a)do they
b)didn't they
c)don't they
d)did they
The main clause "They used to laugh" uses simple past tense with "used to" (past). The negative question tag for past tense is "didn't they?" — positive statement takes a negative tag.
Q16Idiom / Phrase
The idiom "two-faced" means:
a)having two different opinions on minor matters
b)saying one thing while meaning another; being deceitful
c)being extremely good-looking from every angle
d)having the ability to perform two tasks at once
"Two-faced" means being deceitful — presenting a friendly face publicly while harbouring different feelings privately. This is precisely what the poem describes in every stanza: smiling faces that conceal cold calculation.
Q17Figure of Speech
Identify the figure of speech in the line: "My laugh in the mirror shows only my teeth like a snake's bare fangs."
a)Metaphor
b)Simile
c)Personification
d)Alliteration
This is a simile — the narrator's bared-teeth smile is compared to a snake's fangs using the word "like." A metaphor would say "my teeth ARE fangs"; a simile says "my teeth are LIKE fangs."
Q18Content
In the poem "Once Upon a Time," the narrator asks his son to teach him how to:
a)shake hands properly
b)wear the right face
c)laugh genuinely
d)say goodbye
The final plea of the poem is "show me, son, how to laugh" — the narrator wants to relearn how to laugh genuinely with his heart and eyes, the way he did "once upon a time" when he was a child like his son.
Q19Content
The poet Gabriel Okara was awarded the Commonwealth Poetry Award for his outstanding contribution to:
a)Drama
b)Poetry
c)Fiction
d)Journalism
Gabriel Okara was honoured with the Commonwealth Poetry Award — recognising his distinguished contribution to the world of poetry. His poem "The Call of the River Nun" also won the best award for Literature at the Nigerian Festival of Arts in 1953.
Q20Content
The phrase "once upon a time" in the poem refers to:
a)a specific historical event in Nigeria
b)the narrator's future aspirations
c)a past time when people were genuine and warm
d)the narrator's childhood stories
"Once upon a time" refers to a past era — when people laughed with their hearts, shook hands with sincerity, and expressed genuine warmth. The narrator uses this fairy-tale opening to signal that such authentic human connection is now lost, belonging only to memory.
