About the Author

Let us meet Mangte Chungneijang Mary Kom, the sportsperson whose life is not just a success story, but a lesson in courage. The textbook introduces her as a boxer, the winner of five World Boxing Championships, and an Olympic Bronze Medal winner in 2012. That means we are not reading about someone who won once by luck; we are reading about a champion who returned again and again to the ring, faced new opponents, and proved herself repeatedly.

As a school girl, Mary Kom played a variety of sports — hockey, football, and athletics. This detail matters because it shows that her body and mind were trained by movement, discipline, and competition even before boxing entered her life. The textbook says she was inspired by Manipuri boxer Dingko Singh, who won a gold at the Asian Games in 1998. Think about that moment: one athlete’s victory lit a fire in another young athlete’s heart.

Mary Kom took up boxing and slowly built a career that moved from struggle to recognition. Her first international medal was a silver in Pennsylvania, USA, in 2001. She later became the only woman boxer to have won a medal in each one of the six World Championships. The textbook tells us that she was honoured with the Padma Shri in 2010 and the Padma Bhushan in 2013 for her achievements in sports. She wrote her autobiography Unbreakable in 2013.

What makes her writing distinctive is its honesty. She does not pretend that victory is easy. She tells us about money worries, unfamiliar food, jet lag, weight loss, disappointment, public felicitation, and the hunger to win gold. This is why the extract feels alive: it is not a speech from a stage; it is the memory of a boxer who has paid the cost of every medal.

Why this matters for this text: This lesson is an autobiographical extract, so every event comes through Mary Kom’s own experience. When she says she cried after losing gold, we understand her ambition. When she says the silver made her believe she could take on any boxer, we understand her growth. Knowing Mary Kom helps us see that “The Queen of Boxing” is not only about medals; it is about confidence earned through struggle.

Introduction / Before We Read

Imagine being selected to represent your country, but not having enough money for the trip. Imagine reaching a foreign country after a long flight, tired, hungry, and nervous, while your opponents are already prepared. Would you still step into the ring and say, “I can face anyone”? That is the emotional world of this lesson.

This text is an autobiographical extract from Mary Kom’s Unbreakable. In simple words, Mary Kom herself is telling us what happened during her boxing career. We read not only the facts of tournaments and medals, but also her fear, disappointment, hunger, gratitude, and determination.

The textbook prepares us by saying that autobiographies of accomplished sportspersons are full of challenges and single-minded pursuit. That is exactly what this lesson gives us. It begins just after the Bangkok championship, when Mary Kom was selected in the 48 kg category for the International Boxing Association World Women’s Boxing Championships in Pennsylvania, USA, in November–December 2001.

Soon after the Bangkok championship, I was selected in the 48kg category for the International Boxing Association (originally the Association Internationale de Boxe Amateur, or the AIBA) World Women’s Boxing Championships in Pennsylvania, USA, in November–December 2001.

This opening immediately places Mary Kom on the world stage. The key detail is 48kg category, because later we understand how losing appetite and weight affected her final bout.

Themes and Literary Devices
Theme

Determination grows through struggle. Mary Kom begins the lesson with financial anxiety, then faces jet lag, unfamiliar food, tough opponents, and the pain of losing gold. Yet she carries away the conviction that she can face any boxer. In an essay, write that the lesson presents success as a process of fighting fear, learning from defeat, and returning stronger.

Theme

Financial difficulty should not defeat talent. Mary Kom’s father could collect only Rs. 2,000. Onler, students, elders, and two MPs helped her gather money. This shows how many sportspersons, especially from modest backgrounds, need support before they can even compete. For an exam answer, connect this to the paragraph question on lack of sponsorship.

Theme

Recognition brings responsibility. Mary Kom receives garlands, drumbeats, dancing, a victory ride, felicitation, and praise. But she is not satisfied with silver. She speaks of her hope to win gold in future tournaments. The lesson teaches that true champions treat praise not as an ending, but as motivation.

Autobiographical voice
“I kept telling myself, ‘I can face anyone in the ring.’”

This line works because it comes directly from Mary Kom’s inner voice. It lets us hear the self-belief forming inside her.

Contrast
“I was the only one in the team to get a medal. But the biggest thing I took away from this championship was the conviction...”

The contrast between medal and conviction is powerful. The silver is visible, but the confidence is the deeper victory.

Metaphorical phrase
“The fight and all that followed are clearly etched in my memory.”

“Etched” suggests something carved permanently. It shows that her first international medal was unforgettable.
